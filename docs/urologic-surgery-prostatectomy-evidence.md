# Urologic Surgery — Laparoscopic Radical Prostatectomy: Evidence Review

Research conducted 2026-09-24 for the new `/urologic-surgery/laparoscopic-radical-prostatectomy`
pillar. Purpose: validate/refine the owner's working-hypothesis benchmark
ranges for the "Outcomes depend on more than the platform" comparison
section before any page copy is written. Every number that will appear
on the live page must trace back to a row in this document.

**Status: Phase A research — not yet owner-reviewed. Ranges below are
proposed, not final.**

## Method

Search strategy prioritized (per brief): systematic reviews/meta-analyses
first, then RCTs, then large high-volume series, then learning-curve
studies specifically demonstrating surgeon-experience effects, cross-
checked against EAU guideline direction. Marketing/clinic pages were not
used as evidence. Sources were located via PubMed/PMC, Europe PMC, and
publisher sites; paywalled full texts were supplemented with the open
abstract/PMC record where the full text itself was inaccessible — noted
per source below.

**Important limitation of this review**: this was a targeted literature
search to validate specific numeric ranges, not an exhaustive systematic
review in its own right. Several cells below (marked) rely on one or two
strong sources rather than pooled meta-analytic estimates, because a
cleanly isolated figure matching Dr. Molina's exact outcome definition
was not always available in the time available. These gaps are flagged
explicitly rather than papered over with invented precision.

## Source register

### S1 — Kim DK, Moon YJ, Chung DY, et al.
**"Comparison of Robot-Assisted, Laparoscopic, and Open Radical
Prostatectomy Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis
from KSER Update Series."** *Medicina (Kaunas)*. 2025;61(1):61.
- **Study type**: Network meta-analysis (Bayesian hierarchical random-effects)
- **Sample size**: 80 studies, 62,158 patients (RARP/LRP/ORP combined)
- **Approach**: RARP vs LRP vs ORP (three-way)
- **Outcome definitions**: continence and potency definitions **varied
  across included studies** — authors explicitly flag this as a
  limitation, not resolved by the pooling
- **Follow-up**: variable across included studies (not uniformly reported in abstract)
- **Result**:
  - Continence: RARP vs ORP no significant difference (RR 1.057); LRP vs ORP no significant difference (RR 0.921)
  - Potency: RARP significantly better than ORP (RR 1.201) and LRP (RR 1.438)
  - PSM: RARP significantly lower than ORP (RR 0.893); LRP vs ORP no significant difference (RR 0.878)
  - Major complications (Clavien ≥III): RARP significantly fewer than ORP (RR 0.493); LRP vs ORP no significant difference (RR 0.826)
- **Limitations (authors' own)**: only 6/80 studies were RCTs; "high
  heterogeneity due to differences in surgical techniques, surgeon skill
  and experience" — i.e., the pooled RR already contains unmeasured
  surgeon-volume effects.
- Access: PMC (open), full text read.

### S2 — Cao L, Yang Z, Qi L, Chen M.
**"Robot-assisted and laparoscopic vs open radical prostatectomy in
clinically localized prostate cancer: perioperative, functional, and
oncological outcomes."** *Medicine (Baltimore)*. 2019;98(22):e15770.
- **Study type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis, 2 RCTs + 9 prospective comparative studies
- **Sample size**: 8,522 patients (5,051 RARP/LRP pooled; 3,471 ORP)
- **Approach**: RARP + LRP pooled together vs ORP (does not separate RARP from LRP)
- **Outcome definitions**: continence = "no pad use or no leakage," most studies; potency definitions varied across studies (explicitly noted as a limitation)
- **Follow-up**: 12 months
- **Result**:
  - Continence 12mo: 71.6% (RARP/LRP) vs 70.8% (ORP), n.s.
  - Potency 12mo: 14.6% (RARP/LRP) vs 20.3% (ORP), n.s. — **note: this
    is an all-comer cohort, not restricted to previously-potent,
    bilateral-nerve-sparing patients, so it is NOT directly comparable
    to Dr. Molina's 74% figure (see Important Note on Potency Comparability below)**
  - PSM overall: 22.3% (RARP/LRP) vs 28.6% (ORP), n.s.
  - PSM ≤pT2: 14.7% vs 18.8%, n.s.
  - PSM ≥pT3: 41.4% vs 50.1%, n.s.
  - Major complications (Clavien III–V): **3.3%** (RARP/LRP) vs 8.5% (ORP)
- **Limitations (authors' own)**: only 2 RCTs; "almost impossible to
  assess the impact of surgeon ability"; variable nerve-sparing technique
  across studies.
- Access: PMC (open), full text read.

### S3 — Ficarra V, Novara G, Rosen RC, et al.
**"Systematic review and meta-analysis of studies reporting urinary
continence recovery after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy."**
*Eur Urol*. 2012;62(3):405–417.
- **Study type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Approach**: RARP only (no LRP/ORP comparator arm in this specific paper)
- **Outcome definition**: "no pad" continence
- **Follow-up**: 12 months
- **Result**: 12-month "no pad" continence rate averaged **84%**, with
  reported study-level rates ranging 69–96% (i.e., "no pad" incontinence
  reported 4–31% across studies, mean 16%)
- **Limitations**: this is the classic reference series but is now 14
  years old (data collected pre-2011); continence definitions and
  patient-reported vs. surgeon-reported assessment varied across pooled studies.
- Access: abstract/secondary citation only — publisher full text paywalled.

### S4 — Ficarra V, Novara G, Ahlering TE, et al.
**"Systematic review and meta-analysis of studies reporting potency
rates after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy."** *Eur Urol*.
2012;62(4):418–430.
- **Study type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Approach**: RARP only
- **Outcome definition**: potency, variably defined across pooled studies (heterogeneous — one of the paper's own stated limitations)
- **Follow-up**: 12 months
- **Result**: 12-month potency rates ranged **54–90%** across pooled studies
- **Limitations**: wide range reflects genuinely heterogeneous
  definitions (some studies all-comer, some bilateral-nerve-sparing-only,
  some requiring medication-free erections, some allowing PDE5i) — the
  paper's own point is that this heterogeneity makes a single pooled
  number misleading, which is exactly the caution this project's brief
  asked to respect.
- Access: abstract/secondary citation only — publisher full text paywalled.

### S5 — Novara G, Ficarra V, Mocellin S, et al.
**"Systematic review and meta-analysis of studies reporting oncologic
outcome after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy."** *Eur Urol*.
2012;62(3):382–404.
- **Study type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Approach**: RARP only
- **Outcome definitions**: PSM, all-comer and stage-stratified
- **Result**: Overall PSM **15%** (all-comers); **9%** in pathologically
  organ-confined (pT2) disease
- **Limitations**: pT3-specific pooled figure not confirmed directly from
  this paper in this review (see S6 for a pT3 figure from a related
  multi-institutional dataset instead — do not conflate the two sources).
- Access: abstract/secondary citation only — publisher full text paywalled.

### S6 — Multi-institutional RARP margin study (cited via S5-adjacent literature)
**Positive surgical margin rate by pathologic stage after RARP: pT2
9.45%, pT3 37.2%** (Journal of Urology multi-institutional dataset,
referenced alongside the Novara 2012 series).
- **Limitations**: this pT3 figure comes from a separate multi-
  institutional cohort, not the Novara 2012 pooled meta-analysis itself
  — flagged here as a distinct, lower-confidence data point rather than
  silently merged with S5. Needs independent verification before being
  presented as a "meta-analytic" pT3 RARP figure on the live page; safer
  to present as "reported in large multi-institutional series."

### S7 — Novara G, Ficarra V, Rosen RC, et al.
**"Systematic review and meta-analysis of perioperative outcomes and
complications after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy."** *Eur
Urol*. 2012;62(3):431–452.
- **Study type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Approach**: RARP only, with cumulative comparison to ORP/LRP
- **Result**: Mean overall complication rate **9%**, "most... low grade";
  most common individual complications: lymphocele/lymphorrhea (3.1%),
  urine leak (1.8%), reoperation (1.6%)
- **Limitations**: the paper's own abstract (as retrieved) does not
  break out a single pooled Clavien ≥III percentage — "most being low
  grade" is qualitative, not a number we can cite as "X% Clavien ≥III
  in this paper." The Clavien ≥III figure used in the working range
  below instead leans on S2 (Cao 2019, 3.3% RARP/LRP pooled major
  complications), which does report Clavien-graded major complications directly.
- Access: abstract/secondary citation only — publisher full text paywalled.

### S8 — Rassweiler J, Stolzenburg J, Sulser T, et al.
**"Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy — the experience of the German
Laparoscopic Working Group."** *Eur Urol*. 2006;49(1):113–119.
- **Study type**: Multicenter retrospective series
- **Sample size**: 5,824 patients, 18 centers, 50 surgeons (3 centers >500 cases, 6 centers >250 cases — genuinely high-volume/expert tier)
- **Approach**: LRP only
- **Outcome definitions**: continence not further specified in the
  retrieved abstract beyond "continence at 12 months"; potency = "full
  erections" after bilateral nerve-sparing — **note this reads as a
  stricter definition than Dr. Molina's "penetration with or without
  PDE5i," which is why S8's potency figure is markedly lower than S9's
  (see Important Note on Potency Comparability below)**
- **Follow-up**: 12 months
- **Result**:
  - Continence 12mo: 84.9% (range 72–94% across centers)
  - Potency 12mo (bilateral NS, "full erections"): 52.5% (range 35–67%)
  - PSM pT2: 10.6%; PSM pT3a: 32.7%
  - 5-year biochemical-recurrence-free rate: pT2 91.4%, pT3a 82.5%
  - Mean operative time: 211 min; conversion to open: 2.4%; overall complication rate: 8.9% (bleeding 0.3–2.5%, rectal injury 1.5–2.5%) — **all-grade, not Clavien-stratified**
- **Limitations**: 1999–2004 cohort (now 20+ years old); potency
  definition stricter/different from Dr. Molina's; complication rate is
  all-grade, so it likely overstates the Clavien ≥III subset if used
  directly for that comparison.
- Access: citation + abstract data confirmed via search; not full-text read.

### S9 — Rassweiler J, Wagner AA, Moazin M, Gözen AS, Teber D, Frede T, Su LM.
**"Anatomic nerve-sparing laparoscopic radical prostatectomy:
comparison of retrograde and antegrade techniques."** *Urology*.
2006;68(3):587–591.
- **Study type**: Two-center comparative case series
- **Sample size**: >500 bilateral/unilateral nerve-sparing procedures across Heilbronn (retrograde technique) and Baltimore (antegrade technique)
- **Approach**: LRP only, two expert/high-volume centers
- **Outcome definition**: "questionnaire-based potency rate," bilateral
  nerve-sparing, intercourse achieved **with or without PDE5 inhibitors**
  — **this is the closest definitional match in this review to Dr.
  Molina's own stated definition**
- **Result**: Potency 67% (Heilbronn, retrograde) and 76% (Baltimore, antegrade)
- **Limitations**: questionnaire-based (self-report) rather than a
  validated instrument like SHIM in all cases per the retrieved
  abstract; two-center, not a large multicenter pool; findings framed
  around technique comparison, not primarily a volume/experience study,
  though both centers were unambiguously high-volume for their era.
- Access: Europe PMC abstract record, confirmed.

### S10 — Vickers AJ, Bianco FJ, Serio AM, et al.
**"The surgical learning curve for laparoscopic radical prostatectomy:
a retrospective cohort study."** *Lancet Oncol*. 2009;10(5):475–480.
- **Study type**: Retrospective cohort, multi-surgeon, multi-institution
- **Sample size**: 4,702 patients, 29 surgeons, 7 institutions (Europe + North America), 1998–2007
- **Approach**: LRP only
- **Outcome definition**: biochemical recurrence risk as a function of
  cumulative surgeon case number (not continence/potency/PSM directly)
- **Result**: Increasing surgical experience associated with substantial
  reductions in cancer recurrence risk; the learning curve for LRP was
  found to accrue **more slowly** than the published open-surgery
  learning curve, i.e., it takes a laparoscopic surgeon meaningfully
  more cases to reach a given oncologic-outcome plateau than an open surgeon.
- **Limitations**: outcome is biochemical recurrence, not PSM/continence/
  potency directly — used here to support the *general* "learning curve
  is real and specifically slower for laparoscopy" claim, not as a
  numeric benchmark input for any specific table cell.
- Access: abstract confirmed via search; full text not read (paywalled).

### S11 — Secin FP, Savage C, Abbou C, et al.
**"The learning curve for laparoscopic radical prostatectomy: an
international multicenter study."** *J Urol*. 2010;184(6):2291–2296.
- **Study type**: Retrospective multicenter cohort
- **Sample size**: 8,544 patients, 51 surgeons, 14 academic institutions (Europe + US)
- **Approach**: LRP only
- **Outcome definition**: positive surgical margin as a function of cumulative surgeon experience
- **Result**: Overall PSM 22% (1,862/8,544); positive-margin risk
  improved with experience up to a **plateau at 200–250 prior surgeries**;
  absolute risk difference for 10 vs. 250 prior surgeries = 4.8% (95% CI 1.5–8.5)
- **Limitations**: margins only, no functional (continence/potency)
  learning-curve data in this paper; pooled 22% figure spans surgeons at
  all experience levels, so it is a blend rather than a clean
  "beginner-only" number.
- Access: abstract confirmed via search; full text not read (paywalled).

### S12 — Mitre AI, Chammas MF Jr, Rocha JEA Jr, et al.
**"Laparoscopic Radical Prostatectomy: The Learning Curve of a Low
Volume Surgeon."** *ScientificWorldJournal*. 2013;2013:974276.
- **Study type**: Single-surgeon retrospective case series
- **Sample size**: 165 patients, single low-volume surgeon (prior laparoscopic experience in other procedures, no direct RP mentorship), 2003–2011
- **Approach**: LRP only
- **Outcome definition**: PSM by consecutive case tertile (Group A: cases 1–55; B: 56–110; C: 111–165)
- **Result**:
  - PSM: Group A 29.1%, Group B 21.8%, Group C 5.5% (p=0.02)
  - Operative time, blood loss, and transfusion rate all improved significantly from Group A→C; outcomes "stabilized after 110 cases"
  - Conversions to open: 4/55 in Group A only; 0 thereafter
- **Limitations**: continence and erectile function were **explicitly
  not evaluated** in this paper ("due to lack of standardization") —
  this is the specific gap behind this review's lower confidence in the
  learning-curve continence/potency estimates in the working table below.
- Access: PMC (open), full text read.

### S13 — Madi R, Madi N, Ei Bitar J, Tahir J, Shbair Z, Mukherji D.
**Retzius-sparing robotic radical prostatectomy, UAE regional series.**
*J Robotic Surg*. 2026;20(1):390.
- **Study type**: Single-center retrospective case series
- **Sample size**: 100 patients, Clemenceau Medical Center, Dubai, June 2021–December 2024
- **Approach**: RARP only (Retzius-sparing technique specifically)
- **Result**: Continence 78% immediate, rising to 97% on follow-up; PSM
  27% (apical in 40% of PSM cases); pT2 61%, advanced (pT3+) 39% of
  cohort; median console time 94 min; median blood loss 100 mL; "minor
  complications in a few cases, no intraoperative complications"; potency not reported
- **Why included**: this is the only UAE-specific published outcomes
  series located, relevant to the page's Abu Dhabi/UAE audience and
  competitive context (Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi and Burjeel Cancer
  Institute both publicly advertise robotic prostatectomy in the same
  market).
- **Limitations**: small series (n=100), single relatively new program
  (39% advanced-stage case mix may explain the higher PSM than the
  larger international series above); should be cited as regional
  context, not as a "high-volume expert" benchmark tier — it does not
  belong in the same tier as S3/S4/S5/S7 (much larger pooled RARP data).
- Access: Europe PMC abstract record, confirmed.

### S14 — EAU Guidelines on Prostate Cancer, 2026 edition (Uroweb)
- **Study type**: Clinical practice guideline (living document, annual update)
- **Relevant statement**: RALP is described as the preferred minimally
  invasive technique over open retropubic prostatectomy; guideline

- **Use in this project**: guideline-level framing only — this review
  does not extract guideline recommendation grades verbatim and does not

  guidelines emphasize technique/volume factors, consistent with this
  page's core message).
- Access: uroweb.org guideline page, summary only.

## Important note on potency comparability

This review deliberately did **not** force every potency figure above
into one column, because the sources use materially different
definitions and cohorts:

- **All-comer potency** (any patient, regardless of baseline function or
  nerve-sparing status) — S2's 14.6%/20.3% figures are this type, and
  are **not usable** as a comparator for Dr. Molina's 74% figure.
- **Bilateral nerve-sparing, strict/"full erection" definition** — S8's
  52.5% (range 35–67%) is this type.
- **Bilateral nerve-sparing, "intercourse possible with or without
  PDE5i"** — S9's 67–76% and Dr. Molina's own 74% are this type, and
  are the fairest direct comparison in this review.
- **RARP pooled, heterogeneous definitions** — S4's 54–90% range spans
  multiple definition types by the authors' own admission.

The proposed benchmark table (below, and on the eventual page) must
carry this same distinction explicitly rather than presenting a single
flattened "potency %" per tier.

## What this means for the page's core claim

The evidence above supports, and does **not** overstate, the brief's
required positioning:

> "Robotic technology can offer technical advantages [S1: potency RR
> 1.438 vs LRP; S1: fewer major complications vs ORP], but published
> outcomes vary substantially between surgeons and centres [S1's own
> stated heterogeneity limitation; the 35–90% potency spread across S4,
> S8, S9 for ostensibly similar bilateral-nerve-sparing cohorts], and
> high-volume laparoscopic series can achieve oncological and functional
> outcomes within the range reported by contemporary high-volume robotic
> programs [S9's 67–76% potency vs S4's 54–90% RARP range; S8's 84.9%
> continence vs S3's 84% RARP average; S12's most-experienced tertile
> PSM 5.5% vs S5's 9% RARP pT2 figure]."

Every clause of the required core statement is directly traceable to a
specific source above. No claim of laparoscopic superiority, and no
claim that technique doesn't matter, is supported or intended by this
evidence base.

## Outstanding gaps for a follow-up literature pass (before publication)

1. A dedicated learning-curve study reporting **continence and potency**
   (not just PSM) by case-number tertile would materially strengthen the
   🟠-flagged rows above. S12 explicitly did not measure these; a
   different single- or multi-surgeon learning-curve paper reporting
   functional outcomes should be sought.
2. Full-text access to S3, S4, S5, S7 (all paywalled, worked from
   abstract/secondary citation only) to confirm exact denominators and
   any subgroup breakdowns (e.g., bilateral-nerve-sparing-only potency
   within S4's 54–90% range) before the numbers are locked into page copy.
3. A more recent (post-2018) large RARP series for "contemporary"
   framing — S3/S4/S5/S7 are all 2012 publications analyzing pre-2011
   data; the KSER 2025 network meta-analysis (S1) is genuinely
   contemporary but pools RARP/LRP/ORP rather than reporting RARP-only
   absolute rates, so it corroborates direction/relative-risk but not
   the absolute-percentage ranges used in the table.
